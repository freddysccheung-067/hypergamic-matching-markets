# Attribution

Generated by `scripts/run_attribution.py`. Do not edit by hand — re-run it after any change to the weights, the trait distributions, or the heterogeneity knobs.

Configuration: `population.n = 1000`, seeds 42–48, alpha = 1.0, beta = 2.0, gamma = 0.3, lambda = 1.5, delta = 0.5, taste_sigma = 0.5, shock_scale = 1.5. Shortlist size k = 20; each side has 500 candidates.

Every figure is the mean ± sample sd over the seeds, not a single run. The sd is the spread *between markets*, which is what matters: a candidate appears in many shortlists, so each market contributes thousands of pooled pairs and its within-market binomial error is negligible, while the identity of the endogenous top of the market changes with the draw. The top-10 row is where this bites hardest — its per-market values span a factor of six.

## 1. Generative correlation

Correlation across all agents, before any selection. Status, attractiveness, sociability and age are drawn independently by `sample_population`, so every entry here should be zero up to sampling error (about ±0.03 at this N). This is the null against which the next table is read.

| trait pair | population correlation |

| age / sociability | +0.010 ± 0.025 |
| age / status | -0.012 ± 0.024 |
| attractiveness / sociability | -0.011 ± 0.027 |
| attractiveness / status | +0.008 ± 0.010 |
| sociability / status | +0.013 ± 0.032 |

## 2. Selection-induced correlation

Correlation across pooled (agent, candidate) pairs where the candidate is in the agent's top-k, counting a candidate once per shortlist it appears on. This is what an analyst would see computing a correlation from a swipe log or a table of matched pairs.

Read this against table 1, never on its own. A non-zero entry here is a property of the *search*, not of the people: the market ranks on several traits at once, and a shortlist is a surviving subset of that ranking. The entry for status and attractiveness is the one the section is named after.

| trait pair | top-10 | top-100 | top-500 (whole pool) |
|---|---|---|---|
| age / attractiveness | +0.028 ± 0.079 | +0.019 ± 0.037 | +0.002 ± 0.026 |
| age / sociability | -0.006 ± 0.083 | +0.002 ± 0.052 | +0.010 ± 0.025 |
| age / status | +0.035 ± 0.086 | +0.009 ± 0.039 | -0.012 ± 0.024 |
| attractiveness / sociability | +0.041 ± 0.072 | +0.001 ± 0.040 | -0.011 ± 0.027 |
| attractiveness / status | -0.248 ± 0.125 | -0.177 ± 0.050 | +0.008 ± 0.010 |
| sociability / status | +0.018 ± 0.126 | +0.012 ± 0.073 | +0.013 ± 0.032 |

The last column is the whole candidate pool, so it reproduces table 1 to the digit. That is the check that the two estimators agree when selection is removed.

Direction: the induced correlation at small k is **negative**, and it decays toward zero as k widens. That is the signature of selecting on two traits jointly — among candidates that clear a high bar, a higher status comes with a lower attractiveness on average, because a candidate can be admitted on either. A positive value here would need a different mechanism explaining it, and the sign should be re-derived rather than assumed if a retune flips it.

## 3. Leave-one-term-out

Share of the agent's top-20 that survives when one term's weight is set to zero, averaged over agents and seeds. Share-in-common, `|A ∩ B| / k` — not Jaccard. 1.000 means the term does not move the shortlist at all; low means the term drives it.

| term zeroed | weight | overlap (shipped chemistry) | overlap (chemistry off) |
|---|---|---|---|
| status | `alpha` | +0.748 ± 0.010 | +0.529 ± 0.049 |
| attract | `beta` | +0.607 ± 0.014 | +0.375 ± 0.034 |
| distance | `gamma` | +0.929 ± 0.005 | +0.908 ± 0.016 |
| indicator | `lambda` | +0.918 ± 0.002 | +0.910 ± 0.014 |

Ranked by effect at the shipped weights: `attract` (0.607), `status` (0.748), `indicator` (0.918), `distance` (0.929).

**The ordering is a property of these weights, not of the terms.** Each number is one weight set to zero while the others stay put, on one pair of trait distributions. Two consequences follow, and both matter for how this table is cited:

- The weight decides. `beta` is twice `alpha` at the shipped config, so attractiveness has twice the linear influence and removing it moves more of the shortlist. This table measures the configuration, not an intrinsic ranking of traits.
- The distribution decides. Status is lognormal and heavy-tailed; attractiveness is normal and clipped. A Pareto tail would make status look more powerful still. Change either distribution and re-measure.

The second column is the same ablation with the pairwise chemistry switched off. Comparing the two isolates how much of each term's apparent influence is surviving against unlearnable noise rather than against the other terms.

## 4. Delta power

Share of each **woman's** top-k held in common between delta = 0.0 and delta = 1.0. 1.000 means delta is inert: the indicator fires identically on every candidate in her shortlist, so sweeping delta measures nothing.

Women only, and that restriction is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. The indicator enters the acceptor's utility alone (`Agent::utility_toward`), so a man's ranking is invariant to delta by construction. Pooling both sides would average in a half that contributes exactly 1.0 to every figure — measured 0.846 on the women against 0.923 pooled at the shipped weights, the latter reporting a live indicator as nearly inert. `tests/python/test_attribution.py` pins the invariance directly.

| shortlist size | overlap | inert? |
|---|---|---|
| top-20 | +0.846 ± 0.007 | no |
| top-100 | +0.884 ± 0.004 | no |
| top-500 (whole pool) | +1.000 ± 0.000 | **yes** |

The whole-pool row is inert by construction and is included as the check: when the shortlist is the entire market, no reweighting can change its membership, so a value other than 1.000 there would mean the overlap is comparing something other than shortlists. Its warning is therefore expected and is excluded from the count below. No non-degenerate configuration was inert at these settings.

Delta's power depends on the indicator bisecting the competitive set: some candidate above a woman's cutoff and some below must both be plausible for her. THEORY.md §2.2 gives the condition as `beta/alpha >= 2`, and `tests/cpp/test_delta_power.cpp` guards it from the C++ side. The row above is the same guard measured from Python.

## 5. What these tables do not license

Nothing here estimates anything about real people. The traits are synthetic draws from declared distributions; the market is a specified mechanism, and these are its properties. In particular:

- Table 2 is not evidence that status and attractiveness are related in life. The generative correlation in table 1 is zero by construction, so the only link between them is the one the shortlisting creates.
- Table 3 is not a ranking of what people care about. It is what this weight set, on these distributions, does to a shortlist.
- Table 4 describes whether delta is testable at this configuration, not whether status surplus matters in general.

See THEORY.md §9 for the identification argument.
