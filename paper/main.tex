\title{How long a learned ranker's exposure gap lasts\\
       in a simulated matching market}

\author{Freddy Shi Chun CHEUNG}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
When a learned ranker decides which candidates each side of a matching market
sees, the concentration it adds to exposure is measured against an oracle that
ranks by true utility, and the answer depends on when it is measured. In a
simulated market of 1000 agents whose top-20 shortlists come from a neural
collaborative filter, the ranker's exposure Gini exceeds the oracle's by
$\peakGap$ at round \peakRound{} and by $\gapNinetyNine$ at round 99, and each of
40 seeds then enters and stays inside a pre-registered margin of 0.002, between
rounds \entryMinAll{} and \entryMaxAll{}. At round 0 the untrained ranker gives
every agent on a side nearly the same twenty candidates. Across training doses on
fresh logs the length of the transient is set by optimizer steps rather than
market rounds (elasticity $\doseElasticity$), but a warm start and a larger
market do not follow that rule, and a ranker trained on data from a market it
does not control closes the gap slightly faster. Because the ranker is trained on
the oracle's own lists, the gap is the rate at which an imitator converges. Over
1000 rounds an exponential with a half-life of \halfLife{} rounds fits best, and
the last fifty rounds average $\longTail$, above zero and inside the margin. When a share of agents is replaced every round
the gap had not closed by round 600: at 0.5, 1 and 2 percent per round it is
$\openGapLow$, $\openGapMid$ and $\openGapHigh$ over rounds 500 to 599, still
falling at the lowest rate and rising at the other two. A companion institutional
comparison at the shipped gate changes sign between a five-round horizon and a
late one, while at an inert gate the five-round comparison is positive.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The model is a simulation with synthetic agents. It makes no claim about real
people or any real platform, and it uses no human data.
