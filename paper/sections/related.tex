\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Feedback loops and long-run fairness.} A system that learns from data
its own decisions produce can mislead itself. \citet{ensign2018} prove that
predictive policing sends police back to the same areas regardless of the true
rate when discovered incidents feed the next allocation. \citet{sinha2016}
recover the ratings a recommender has not influenced by deconvolving its feedback
loop, and \citet{schnabel2016} treat recommendations as treatments to obtain
unbiased estimators under selection. \citet{chaney2018} show by simulation that
training on algorithmically confounded data homogenizes behavior without raising
utility, \citet{mansoury2020} that a feedback loop amplifies popularity bias and
hits minority-group users hardest, and \citet{jiang2019} separate the echo chamber
from the filter bubble and propose ways to slow degeneration. \citet{damour2020}
argue that static or single-step analyses miss long-term consequences and make
simulation the instrument for them; \citet{liu2018} show that even one step of
feedback can turn a fairness criterion harmful. \citet{perdomo2020} give the frame
this paper fits: retraining on a distribution the model's own deployment shapes,
and conditions under which it converges to a performatively stable point. Here the
ranker retrains every round on data its past shortlists shape weakly, and the gap
is the path to that point. The claim that one horizon can mislead is
\citet{damour2020}'s; what is added is a measurement of the transient for a
learned ranker in a matching market, of its length, and of what sets it.

\paragraph{Exposure fairness.} \citet{singh2018} define fair rankings by
constraints on the exposure each item receives, and \citet{biega2018} amortize
attention across a sequence of rankings so individual position bias evens out.
Both design a ranking to meet an exposure target. Here exposure is the number of
shortlists an agent appears on, and the gap is measured against an oracle, not a
target: the oracle's own Gini of about $\giniOracleTail$ is concentration that
comes from preferences, which no fair-exposure constraint was meant to remove.
\citet{fleder2009} show that recommenders built on sales and ratings can reduce
sales diversity, a one-sided analogue of the concentration measured here.

\paragraph{Reciprocal recommenders and matching platforms.} \citet{pizzato2010}
introduce the reciprocal recommender for online dating, \citet{tomita2022}
describe a matching-theory recommender deployed on a dating platform, and
\citet{chen2023} run a matching-model ranker in a field experiment and find less
recommendation inequality and more matches. \citet{kanoria2021} show that a
platform restricting what agents can do can raise welfare, \citet{halaburda2018}
that a platform can compete by limiting choice, \citet{arnosti2021} how design can
cut wasted search and screening, and \citet{rios2021} how a national admissions
clearinghouse was designed and run. \citet{mladenov2020} show that matching each
user to the best provider performs poorly when providers need engagement to stay
viable. Each of these designs or evaluates a ranker or a platform; none measures
how long a learned ranker's effect on exposure takes to settle.

\paragraph{Matching theory.} Deferred acceptance finds a stable matching
\citep{gale1962}; the proposer-optimal stable matching is the acceptor-pessimal
one, and no procedure is both stable and strategy-proof \citep{roth1982,knuth1996}.
\citet{roth1999} show that truncating a list changes outcomes,
\citet{ashlagi2017} that a slight imbalance makes the stable matching essentially
unique, and \citet{akbarpour2020} study markets in which agents arrive and depart.
Under a compatibility constraint that is nested in a scalar status, the minimal
unmatched mass follows from Hall's marriage theorem \citep{hall1935} in its
deficiency form as a supremum over thresholds. Table~\ref{tab:validation} checks
the simulator against these results. A learned top-$k$ visibility constraint is
not nested, and no closed form is known for it.

\paragraph{Hypergamy as an economic object.} \citet{saintpaul2015} derives
hypergamous matching from a trade-off in which women forgo more attractive mating
opportunities to credibly signal legitimate children; \citet{almas2023} use
parental earnings rank as a predetermined measure and find hypergamy an important
feature of matching in Norway; \citet{esteve2016} show the reversal of the
educational gender gap moving marriage toward hypogamy with no change in
preferences required. This paper's status bonus is a stylized version of the
preference these works study, and nothing here tests them.

\paragraph{Simulation methodology.} \citet{windrum2007} and \citet{fagiolo2007}
set out why validating an agent-based model is a family of practices rather than
a single test. This paper claims verification against closed forms, not
validation against data. Its equivalence test is the two one-sided tests
procedure of \citet{lakens2017}.
