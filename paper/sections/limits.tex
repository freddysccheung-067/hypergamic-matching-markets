\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Ordered by how much each constrains what the results can mean, not by how easy
it is to fix.

\textbf{Every parameter is a modeling choice.} Nothing is calibrated to data:
the trait distributions are drawn for tractability, the taste and chemistry
parameters have no empirical anchor, and neither the signalling cost, the
defection penalty, nor the entry filter is estimated. The results bound what this
mechanism \emph{can} do under a stated utility and say nothing about whether any
population sits at these parameters. Because traits are $z$-scored before
entering a utility, only rank order matters, but a distribution with no variance would
still destroy its term, so the defaults are deliberately not
self-report-shaped and the attractiveness result is an upper bound on its
influence rather than an estimate. The mitigation is that the two most
consequential knobs are reported as a function of their value. It is partial: a
sweep over four knobs is not a calibration.

\textbf{Five seeds carry most headlines, twenty carry six.} The power analysis
says five is adequate for sign and inadequate for width on two quantities, and
both matter. An
interval on a model's seed-averaged quantity is not an interval on the model's
answer. The five seeds were also not chosen for this purpose; they predate the
twenty-seed run, which shows them to be an unusually low-variance block for two
of the seven tracked quantities (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S2.2). That finding is
reported and is not repaired: the interval arithmetic every table uses was
changed to the construction with nominal small-sample coverage, but the seeds
themselves were left alone, so any claim whose variance happens to be
underestimated by this particular block still reads as tighter than it is. The
honest reading is that the two affected quantities, algorithmic loss and clearing rate,
carry no headline here, and that a future study should choose
seeds for the purpose rather than inherit them.

The H4 optimum carries a sharper caution than a multiplicity correction can
address. It was selected from forty cells on mean \emph{and} low variance, and
Bonferroni corrects the first without undoing the second: on fresh seeds the cell
replicates its rank and loses most of its claimed precision, its seed sd rising
from $\optWelfareSd$ to $\hFourFreshSd$. And the whole grid was measured in a gate regime that does not
respond to the market, which is not the configuration this paper otherwise runs.
At the shipped gate no cell of the forty beats Environment A at all, so the
interior optimum is a result about a regime rather than about the model
(\S\ref{sec:h4regime}).

\textbf{The committed 300-epoch run does not reproduce.} Its NCF arm cannot be
regenerated from this code at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 or 24 threads, nor from the
pre-optimization source at 24; its other three arms regenerate exactly. The
divergence is at the seventh decimal at epoch 5 and grows through the feedback
loop, which is the signature of a different reduction order rather than a
different model (\texttt{docs/REVIEW\_RESPONSE.md}, A3). The convergence numbers
this paper quotes therefore come from a regeneration at one thread rather than
from the committed run; the two agree within tolerances fixed before the
regeneration was read (\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E7). One other archival
directory is in a milder version of the same position: \texttt{rigor/base/}, which
carries the twenty-seed headline, differs from a fresh run at the same thread
count in the fourth decimal.

\textbf{The thread count is part of the run's identity, and that was found late.}
Torch's intra-op reductions are not bitwise associative, so the same seed at one
thread and at eight gives different numbers; across a hundred epochs of this
feedback loop the spread across thread counts reaches $\threadSpreadLo$--$\threadSpreadHi$
in exposure Gini, against a between-seed standard deviation of
$\betweenSeedSdTwenty$ over twenty seeds. Every configuration now pins
\texttt{ncf.num\_threads} to~1 and the contract is tested, but the reproducibility
guarantee this project claims is a guarantee at a named thread count, not an
unconditional one (\texttt{docs/INTERFACES.md} \S3;
\texttt{data/processed/rigor/thread\_sensitivity.csv}).

\textbf{One population size carries the headline.} $N = 1000$ is the only size
at which the full comparison was run, because the full-information benchmark
needs an $O(N^2)$ preference matrix and is capped at $N = 5000$. Clearing rate is
not comparable across population sizes at fixed $k$: it falls roughly as $1/N$, and
scaling $k$ to compensate makes the swipe log grow superlinearly.
The clearing figures are properties of $N = 1000$ at $k = 20$ and do not
transport.

\textbf{There is no closed loop through preferences.} The ranker changes who is
visible, not what anyone wants: the preference generator takes no shown-set
argument. A reader looking for the classic feedback story will not find it here.
A different loop exists, through the acceptance threshold, and it is live at the
shipped configuration; but it relaxes within a few epochs, so even the loop that
is present is a transient. This is the largest gap between this model and the
recommender literature it engages, and it is a structural absence rather than a
measurement problem.

\textbf{There is no attrition.} Nobody joins or leaves in response to outcomes,
so there is no selection at the extensive margin and a market that deteriorated
enough to empty out could not show it. This is why one hypothesis is untested
(\S\ref{sec:h6}), and it bounds the welfare reading: a mechanism that repelled
participants would look free.

\textbf{The defection penalty prices defection without deterring it.} It is
charged after a match dissolves; no agent proposes differently, sets a different
threshold, or stays longer because of it. Making it deter would mean feeding it
into the proposal decision, which is a different model whose institutional result has
not been measured.

\textbf{The benchmark is not an achievable institution.} The oracle ranks by true
utility, so every gap measured against it is a distance from an unattainable
bound rather than a choice an agent could make. Conversely, the institutional
comparison scores B against an oracle A, and against an NCF-mediated A the
ordering reverses. Both facts bound what the A/B result means: it is about the
quality of A's visibility, not institutional form in general.

\textbf{Truncation is imposed, not strategic.} Agents receive their top-$k$ list
and do not game it, so the strategic theory of truncation does not apply
directly, and the model cannot say what happens if agents report their lists
instead.

\textbf{Validation establishes verification, not validity.} Agreement with
closed-form results shows the machinery is correct; it establishes nothing about
the recommender, because the deficit those theorems characterize is measured
under a compatibility constraint on a single scalar, which is exactly what makes it
tractable, while this model's constraint is visibility through an estimated
multi-attribute utility. A matching deficit is not evidence that the
recommender's unmatched mass has a closed form. It does not appear to have one.

\textbf{Nothing here is a claim about people.} The intent type has no
instrument, the utility has no estimated weights, and the environments are model
constructs. Neither is ethically superior: B carries an entry filter that
excludes agents, and the model prices that exclusion rather than endorsing it.
