\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In a closed market the concentration a learned ranker adds to exposure is a cost
of its training. Across training doses on fresh logs it is set by the number of optimizer
steps the ranker has taken, not by how long the market has run; a warm start and a
larger market do not follow that rule. A comparison with an oracle at a fixed
number of rounds reads the ranker's training state as much as the ranker, so an
exposure audit of a learned ranker is incomplete without the horizon it was read
at.

In a market whose population turns over, the cost had not ended by round 600 at
any rate tested. The gap is larger when the ranker meets agents it has not learned
more often, stays above the margin, and at the higher rates falls on newcomers and
favors long-tenured agents.
Which of the two describes a platform depends on that rate, which this model
cannot supply. Neither reading covers welfare: the ranker's welfare stays below the oracle's
while its exposure converges, which is consistent with lists that hold the same
agents in a different order but was not tested, and the institutional comparison,
read after the transient, finds a complete-information subgraph better than either
version of the mediated market over a range of sizes.
