\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

This section states what was run, at what sample size, against what controls,
and what was held fixed, so a reader can tell which numbers in
\S\ref{sec:results} are comparable to which and can re-derive any of them from
the shipped configuration without re-running the simulation.

\subsection{Regimes, and why the horizon is a design variable}

The shipped configuration is $N = 1000$ agents, shortlist length $k = 20$, gate
target $\phi = 0.02$, nominal horizon 100 epochs. Five regimes appear, and the
differences between them are results in their own right:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
regime & $\phi$ & epochs & what it isolates \\
\hline
\texttt{inert20} & 0.2 & 20 & gate never binds; market deaf to its own outcomes \\
\texttt{live20} & 0.02 & 20 & gate binds, then relaxes \\
\texttt{live100} & 0.02 & 100 & the shipped horizon \\
\texttt{base20} & 0.02 & 100 & the same horizon, twenty seeds \\
\texttt{long300} & 0.02 & 300 & convergence, not a steady state \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The institutional comparison uses a sixth: $\phi = 0.2$, the inert gate, at five
epochs per paired run. That is the same gate as \texttt{inert20} and it is the
regime in which H4 holds and in which it is refuted at the shipped gate
(\S\ref{sec:h4regime}).

The inert regime is retained rather than discarded because the difference
between it and the live regime is the evidence that the feedback channel matters
at all. The two 20-epoch regimes differ in $\phi$ alone; the two live regimes
differ in horizon alone, and their first epochs agree only approximately. The
four arms of the 300-epoch run and the 100-epoch run are bit-identical through
epoch 4 and then diverge in the NCF arm alone, reaching $3\times10^{-4}$ in
exposure Gini by epoch 19; the other three arms stay bit-identical. That is the
signature of a different floating-point reduction order in the training path, not
of a different model, and it is why every figure in this paper names the run it
came from: the 100-epoch gap is quoted from \texttt{ablation\_h100} and the
300-epoch trajectory from \texttt{rigor/long\_horizon}, which are two runs and
not one run at two lengths. The difference reaches the third decimal by epoch 99
($0.6935$ against $0.6967$) and changes no verdict, but the second directory
cannot be regenerated from this code at any thread count tested, so the
300-epoch trajectory is quoted as the committed measurement rather than as a
reproduction (\texttt{docs/REVIEW\_RESPONSE.md}, A3).

\subsection{Arms}

The A-side ablation compares four arms on a shared population, seed and utility
set, so differences are attributable to the visibility rule rather than to
sampling:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ncf}, the learned ranker. The treatment arm.
\item \textbf{oracle}, which ranks by true utility under the same proposer
  direction and shortlist length. The benchmark, not a deployable institution.
\item \textbf{popularity}, which ranks by aggregate engagement; its exposure Gini
  sits at the analytic closed form, which checks the exposure machinery.
\item \textbf{random}, which ranks uniformly, because distance from uniformity is
  not a measure of harm and without it a reader cannot tell whether the ranker's
  concentration is unusual or what any non-personalised rule does.
\end{itemize}

{\sloppy
The institutional comparison adds Environment B at four signalling costs
($c_B \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25\}$) crossed with ten subgraph sizes
($\phi_B \in \{0.02$, $0.05$, $0.10$, $0.15$, $0.25$, $0.35$, $0.50$, $0.65$,
$0.80$, $1.00\}$): forty cells, five seeds, both accountings. Environment A
there is the \emph{oracle} arm, so nothing is estimated in either environment;
the NCF-mediated comparison is reported separately and reverses the ordering. The
drift decomposition is a $2\times2$ factorial over exogenous drift and
algorithmic mediation, each on or off, from which the three terms are estimated.
\par}

\subsection{Sampling and what counts as a replicate}

Five seeds (42 through 46) underlie the horizon, sweep and institutional results,
and twenty the shipped-horizon ones. This is the design
decision most likely to be objected to, so the response is registered rather than
deferred. A seed is a population, not a run: each draws fresh traits, intents,
alignments and pairwise shocks and holds them fixed across arms, so arms are
compared \emph{paired} by seed and every gap is a per-seed difference.

\textbf{Which quantities rest on twenty seeds and which on five.} So that a
reader is never guessing: the exposure Gini, the NCF--oracle gap, the clearing
rate, rank satisfaction, reach and algorithmic loss at the shipped
hundred-epoch regime are all quoted from a twenty-seed run. Everything else is
five. That includes every horizon claim (the 300-epoch series, its decay fits,
its asymptote and its stationarity tests), every sensitivity-sweep claim (twelve
cells, five seeds each), the institutional grid and its optimum, and the drift
decomposition. A number in \S\ref{sec:results} that is not one of the six named
above is a five-seed number, and the text says so where the distinction could
mislead.

A twenty-seed run (42 through 61) was executed at the shipped regime. A power
calculation against the twenty-seed standard deviation requires fewer than five
seeds to detect every headline effect at 80\% power, with raw requirements below
one in every case, and no headline changes sign or crosses zero at any seed count
from five to twenty (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S2.4). That is a statement about
\emph{sign}, not \emph{width}. Two quantities behave badly on width: the five
seeds are the minimum-variance contiguous block of the sixteen available for
algorithmic loss and second-lowest for clearing, so their standard deviations
understate the population's by roughly a factor of three and two and a half
(\S2.2). Every interval on those two is reported from the twenty-seed run, and no
headline rests on a five-seed interval for either.

\subsection{Intervals, multiplicity and the sweep}

The estimating method is fixed before results are read and differs by seed count
for a measured reason. At five seeds the Student-$t$ interval is quoted; at
twenty, the BCa bootstrap with 10,000 resamples. The BCa interval is reported
beside the $t$ interval at five but never quoted alone, because at that size its
half-width is narrower than the sample's own standard deviation (\texttt{docs/STATS.md}
\S1). Bootstraps are seeded from a fixed constant and regenerate byte for byte.

Two families are corrected. The institutional grid holds 200 rows but 40 distinct
cells (ten sizes by four costs), each replicated over five seeds; a cell is what
was selected over, so its seeds are replicates rather than five further chances
to win, and the family is 40. Uncorrected family-wise error under independence
would be 0.871, but the cells are not independent, since adjacent sizes share
populations and the same seeds are reused, so the realized rate is lower and is
not claimed; the nominal figure is quoted as an upper bound. The decomposition
corrects over its three terms.

Every committed result sits at one setting of four knobs, each swept at three
levels with the others shipped: taste heterogeneity
$\sigma_{\text{taste}} \in \{0.2, 0.5, 1.0\}$, chemistry noise
$\sigma_{\text{shock}} \in \{0.5, 1.5, 3.0\}$, the weight ratio
$\beta/\alpha \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ and the hypergamy margin $\delta \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$:
twelve cells, five seeds, 100 epochs. Four claims were fixed with their
thresholds before the cells were read, so a reader can disagree with the
thresholds rather than only with the verdicts: that the gap excludes zero and is
positive (C1), that its point estimate is at most 0.05 (C2), that the ranker
reaches at least 75\% of agents (C3), and that clearing is non-degenerate (C4).
C2's ceiling and C3's floor are the two judgments most exposed to the charge of
being chosen after the fact, and the only mitigation is that they were written
down first. The shipped cell appears in three axes with identical numbers
($+0.025973$, reach $0.9566$), which checks that the axes are independent.

\subsection{Validation against closed-form results}
\label{sec:validation-design}

Before the recommender is asked anything, the matching and survival-function
machinery is checked against proved results. This is verification, not
validation: agreement with a theorem shows the code is correct and says nothing
about whether the model describes a population.

Two tools are used because the results are of two kinds. The deficit results
concern the largest matching under a \emph{compatibility} constraint, which is a
maximum-cardinality bipartite matching problem and is solved as one; a
stable-matching procedure does not compute it. The uniqueness result is the
stability question and is tested with deferred acceptance.

Table~\ref{tab:validation} reports both. The most informative check is the last:
adding noise of order one percent of the status scale to each agent's own view of
the other side leaves stability intact, with zero blocking pairs in both proposer
directions, while uniqueness fails. The theorem's conclusion therefore rests
on the lists being \emph{common}, not merely correlated with status.

What this licenses is narrow: it shows the deficit and stability machinery is
correct, and does not show the recommender's unmatched mass has a closed form.
The reasons are structural. The deficit is a supremum because the
compatibility constraint is a nested upper set on a single scalar, and a top-$k$
list from an estimated multi-attribute utility has neither property.

\subsection{What was not run}

Three gaps are stated rather than left for discovery. The 300-epoch convergence
run exists only at the shipped cell, so convergence is established at one point
and not across the sweep, which is 100 epochs deep everywhere. The
full-information benchmark materialises an $O(N^2)$ preference matrix and is
capped at $N = 5000$, so larger-population results are NCF-only and lose their
reference. And the model has no attrition mechanism, so one hypothesis cannot be
tested at all (\S\ref{sec:h6}).
