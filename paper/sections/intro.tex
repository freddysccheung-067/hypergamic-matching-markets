\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When a recommender sits between the two sides of a matching market, it decides
which candidates anyone ever sees. The consequence is easy to state and awkward
to price: a preference expressed over a truncated list is not the same
preference that operates over the whole population. The agent on nobody's list
is not rejected; she is never considered, and no preference data will record the
difference.

\subsection*{The measurement problem}

The standard way to measure that harm is to compare exposure under the learned
ranker with exposure under a ranker that is perfect at the same task, on the same
population and seed, and to report the difference at some horizon. This paper
shows that measurement can invert its own conclusion. In the market simulated
here the gap between the learned ranker and an oracle is large and positive at
twenty epochs, small and still positive at a hundred, and indistinguishable from
zero at three hundred. A study that stopped at either of the first two horizons
would report a standing deficiency of learned rankers where what exists is a
start-up cost, and the two are not the same finding: a standing bias is a reason
to regulate the ranker, a transient is a reason to wait.

The inversion is not an artifact of one number being misread. It is what a
single-horizon comparison cannot see, because a decay and a floor look identical
from inside either one. The distinction is measurable, since it takes a longer run and this paper runs one, and establishing that it changes the answer is the
paper's central claim.

\subsection*{The result}

A finite two-sided market of a thousand agents carries a four-dimensional trait
vector and a private commitment type. A neural collaborative filter trained on
the swipe log produces a top-20 shortlist per agent, and a truncated
deferred-acceptance pass clears the market over those shortlists and nothing
else.

\textbf{The gap is a transient.} The learned ranker's exposure Gini exceeds an
oracle's by $\gapBaseTwenty$ $[\gapBaseTwentyLo, \gapBaseTwentyHi]$ at the shipped
hundred-epoch horizon over twenty seeds, positive on all twenty and on all twenty
of a fresh set. Extended to three hundred epochs it peaks at $\gapPeak$ shortly after the
start, is $\gapEpochNinetyNine$ at epoch 99, and is below $0.002$ from about epoch
\firstBelowMarginMin{} to \firstBelowMarginMax{} depending on seed; a pre-declared equivalence test at that margin puts the
tail inside it. What is not established is the limit, since a flat tail is also what a
very slow decay looks like (\S\ref{sec:coldstart}, \S\ref{sec:equivalence}).

\textbf{It is real everywhere but not constant in size.} The gap is positive with
an interval excluding zero in all \nSweepCells{} sweep cells, on both interval
constructions, and reverses nowhere; its magnitude varies by a factor of
\gapRangeRatio{}, so no single
number is ``the'' gap (\S\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

\textbf{The explanation is supported by the one arm that can test it.} The
mechanism offered is that the filter learns whom it can represent. A ranker
pretrained before the loop starts has most of the gap's decay already behind it:
$\warmDecayGapZero$ percent survives, against all of it in the control
(\S\ref{sec:interventions}). Two other tests turn out not to test it. The cell meant
to isolate a market without coverage learning is the same process running more
slowly (reach $\cfourReachFinalShort$ by epoch 299 against $\cfourReachNinetyNineShort$
at epoch 99), and freezing the ranker freezes its shortlists, so any frozen arm is
constant by construction (\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E3).

\textbf{The institutional companion does not survive where the paper runs.} An
interior welfare optimum exists in the gate regime that ignores the market's own
outcomes, and replicates its location on fresh seeds; re-run at the shipped gate,
no cell of the forty beats the global market at any size or cost
(\S\ref{sec:h4regime}, Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}).

\subsection*{What is new}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A horizon dependence that inverts a conclusion.} A single-horizon
  comparison of a recommender's exposure effect cannot distinguish a transient
  from a standing bias, and in this market the distinction reverses the
  interpretation. \S\ref{sec:coldstart}, \S\ref{sec:equivalence}.
\item \textbf{A separation of what replicates from what does not.} Twenty fresh
  seeds that took no part in any earlier choice reproduce every ordering, and
  reproduce the institutional optimum's location while cutting its claimed
  precision by a factor of about five. \S\ref{sec:h3},
  Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}.
\item \textbf{A regime finding that refutes the institutional result where the
  paper actually runs.} The interior optimum is a property of a gate
  configuration that does not respond to the market; at the shipped gate no cell
  of forty beats the global market. \S\ref{sec:h4regime}.
\item \textbf{A mechanism test that supports the coverage reading.} Starting the
  ranker informed removes about $\warmDecayRemovedGapZero$ percent of the gap's
  decay, so the transient is mostly the ranker's untrained state. The arm adds
  gradient steps without adding data, so it does not say which of the two does
  the work. The other arm, a frozen ranker, cannot test anything at all, for a
  reason the paper makes explicit.
  \S\ref{sec:interventions}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Scope, the closed-form context, and what this is not}

Six hypotheses were posed in advance, each with a stated kill condition, and five
kinds of test applied: seed-bootstrapped intervals, a horizon extended to
convergence, a twelve-cell parameter sweep with its thresholds fixed before the
cells were read, multiplicity correction over the forty institutional cells the
headline optimum was selected from, and a replication on seeds chosen for the
purpose rather than inherited. \S\ref{sec:design} states the sample sizes so every
number can be checked.

Nothing here is a claim about a population. Every parameter is a modeling choice,
nothing is calibrated, twenty seeds carry the shipped-horizon quantities and five
carry the horizon, sweep and institutional results, and one population size
carries it. The results bound what this mechanism can do under a stated utility;
\S\ref{sec:limits} says so at length rather than in a footnote.

There is also a specific absence the paper is built around. The constraint here
is \emph{visibility}, not compatibility: the feasible set is the top-$k$ of an
estimated multi-attribute utility, lists differ by agent, and estimator error is
correlated in a way no marginal captures. For a single scalar notion of status
the aggregate questions do have closed-form answers in a recent preprint
\citep{bilderbeek2026}: with unions constrained to be
hypergamous, the minimal unmatched mass is a functional of the two marginal status
distributions alone, and no behavioral strategy changes it. That result depends on
the constraint being a nested upper set, which is what makes the deficit a
one-dimensional supremum, and a learned top-$k$ list has neither property. The
structure that makes the closed form tractable is absent here rather than merely
harder, which is why the question has to be answered numerically, and why the answer turns out to depend on \emph{when} it is asked.
