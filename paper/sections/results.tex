\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results are reported by hypothesis, and the split is deliberate. Two carry the
paper's argument and are given in full here: H3's ordering and the cold-start
result that qualifies it. Five are summarized in one paragraph each, with the full
analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, because their verdicts are weaker and a
reader should be able to weigh the central claim without them. The convergence
result gets its own subsection because it asks about a limit rather than testing a
hypothesis. Table~\ref{tab:ledger} collects the four hypotheses with a committed
interval; H1, H2 and H6 have none, for reasons stated in place.

Two conventions hold. Every interval is a 95\% interval over the seed
distribution, and table~\ref{tab:ledger} prints both the Student-$t$ and the BCa
bounds for every row rather than choosing one silently, because which is
preferred is a small-sample question and the two disagree at five seeds. The
$t$ interval is the one with nominal coverage at n = 5; the BCa bootstrap attains
roughly 84\% there and returns a half-width narrower than the sample's own
standard deviation (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S1). At twenty seeds the two agree to
0.002. Where a row's interval has been widened for multiplicity, which is the H4
row only, the caption and the row both say so: the H4 bounds are Bonferroni-$t$
across the 40 institutional cells, and the uncorrected and Bonferroni-BCa bounds
are beside them in the text (\S\ref{sec:h4}; \texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S5.3). Gaps
are always the paired per-seed difference, never a comparison of two arms'
overlapping intervals.

\subsection{H1, H2, H4, H5, H6: what was tested, and what happened}
\label{sec:appendixpointer}

Four of the six hypotheses are reported in full in Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses},
because their verdicts are weaker than the cold-start result and a reader should
be able to weigh the argument without them. In one paragraph each:

\textbf{H1 (a proposing cost selects on intent).} Direction holds — mean received
intent rises monotonically in $c$ — but the magnitude does not match the closed
form, because the closed form evaluates its threshold at a prior success
probability the market does not supply. Not refuted; no interval registered.
Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, \S H1.

\textbf{H2 (threshold inflation as defence against pooling).} Untestable as
shipped: the $\theta$ update reads only a proposal count, never received intent,
so no path exists by which the predicted slope could appear. Reported as an
absence of mechanism, not as a refutation. Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, \S H2.

\textbf{H4 (an interior institutional optimum).} Refuted at the configuration
this paper runs. At $c_B = 0.01$ in the inert gate regime there is a
non-monotone welfare difference with its optimum at subgraph size $0.65$, which
survives Bonferroni and Holm; re-run at the shipped gate, no cell of the forty
beats the global market at any size or cost. The hypothesis therefore holds in a
regime the rest of the paper does not use and fails in the one it does.
Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, \S H4; \S\ref{sec:h4regime} below.

\textbf{H5 (an interaction term).} The drift-by-mediation interaction is
$\hFiveInteraction$, interval $[\hFiveInteractionLo, \hFiveInteractionHi]$, not
distinguishable from zero, which is what H5 allowed. The algorithmic term
dominates. Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, \S H5.

\textbf{H6 (differential attrition).} Not tested at all: the model has no
attrition margin, so no committed artifact exists and no number is quoted.
Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses}, \S H6.

\subsection{H3: estimation error concentrates exposure}
\label{sec:h3}

\emph{Under test.} On a shared population and seed, the exposure Gini under the
learned ranker exceeds that under an oracle ranking by true utility, because the
ranker cannot represent every agent and those it cannot represent receive no
exposure. Kill condition: the ranker matches or beats the oracle.

\emph{Measured.} Not refuted. At the shipped regime and horizon the paired gap is
$\gapShipped$ with a $t$ interval of $[\gapShippedLo, \gapShippedHi]$, positive on all five
seeds (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S1, row 2; \texttt{intervals.csv}). The twenty-seed
run gives $\gapBaseTwenty$ $[\gapBaseTwentyLo, \gapBaseTwentyHi]$, which is the same quantity on a
superset of the same seeds: seeds 42--46 are the first five of 42--61, so the two
figures agreeing is expected and is not a test of anything. What it does
establish is the interval's width, measured full width against full width: $\gapBaseTwentyBcaWidth$
on the BCa construction at twenty seeds against $\gapLiveBcaWidth$ at five, a factor of about
1.8, and that the point estimate moves by less than its own five-seed interval
when the other fifteen seeds are added. The overlapping-sample agreement is not a
test of anything; \S\ref{sec:sensitivity} is, and the ordering holds in all twelve
of its cells on both interval constructions.

\emph{Refuted?} No in direction; the substantive version, that estimation error is
a \emph{standing} source of concentration, is refuted by \S\ref{sec:coldstart}.
The inequality holds; the mechanism attributed to it does not.

\emph{The replication on fresh seeds.} Everything above rests on seeds 42--61, and
\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S2.2 shows those five are an unusually low-variance block
for two quantities. The gap itself is not one of them, but the objection is
general and the answer is a fresh set. Twenty seeds (100--119) were registered as
a confirmation set before they were run (\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E1) and
executed in the same session, at the same thread count, as a regeneration of
42--61 for comparison. Every quantity listed below is a twenty-seed point
estimate at 1 thread.

The fresh gap is $\gapFresh$ $[\gapFreshLo, \gapFreshHi]$ against $\gapExploratory$
$[\gapExploratoryLo, \gapExploratoryHi]$ on the exploratory set: inside the
pre-registered interval,
and if anything slightly larger. The exposure Gini itself is $\giniNcfFresh$ against
$\giniNcfExploratory$, the oracle level $\giniOracleFresh$ against $\giniOracleExploratory$,
and the arm ordering
(\textsf{popularity} $>$ \textsf{ncf} $>$ \textsf{oracle} $>$ \textsf{random})
holds on all twenty seeds. No quantity moved outside its interval and no ordering
reversed, which is what E1 predicted.

Four measurements of this one quantity now exist, and a reader should know which
is which. The headline quoted above, $\gapShipped$, is five seeds at 24 threads
from \texttt{ablation\_h100/}, the only archival value whose directory reproduces
exactly. The committed twenty-seed interval, $\gapBaseTwenty$
$[\gapBaseTwentyLo, \gapBaseTwentyHi]$, comes from \texttt{rigor/base/}, which does
not regenerate. The two above come from this session at 1 thread. The four are
$+0.0251$, $+0.0237$, $+0.0253$ and $+0.0225$, and the paper keeps the first as
its headline because provenance rather than size is the deciding consideration
(\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E8).

Two differences are worth naming because they bear on how much the seeds were
worth. The fresh set has a smaller spread on the Gini quantities (sd $\giniNcfSdFresh$
against $\giniNcfSdExploratory$ for the NCF level) and a larger one on algorithmic loss
($\algLossSdFresh$ against $\algLossSdExploratory$), which is the same quantity-specific behavior
\S2.2 describes rather than a contradiction of it. And the exploratory set
re-derived here is not bit-identical to the committed \texttt{rigor/base/}: the
seed-42 epoch-99 Gini differs in the fourth decimal. That directory predates the
current code, so the comparison throughout this subsection is against the
regenerated set, not against the committed one.

\emph{What it is not.} The oracle ranks by true utility under the same direction
and shortlist length: an upper bound on what this architecture could achieve, not
a deployable alternative. A gap against an oracle measures how far a learned
ranker falls short, not what a better-designed platform would do.

\subsection{The cold-start result, and its convergence test}
\label{sec:coldstart}

\emph{The question.} H3's gap is positive at every horizon measured. Is it a
floor or a transient? A hundred-epoch run cannot answer, because the gap is still
falling at epoch 99. The run was extended to 300 epochs at the shipped
configuration, five seeds (\texttt{data/processed/rigor/long\_horizon/};
\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S3).

\emph{Trajectory.} The mean gap rises to a peak shortly after the start, then
decays monotonically in the reported points:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrr}
\hline
epoch & 0 & 6 & 19 & 49 & 99 & 124 & 199 & 250--299 \\
gap & $\gapEpochZero$ & $\gapEpoch6$ & $\gapEpoch19$ & $\gapEpoch49$ & $\gapEpoch99$
    & $\gapEpoch124$ & $\gapEpoch199$ & $\tailMean$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The peak is at epoch 6. The hundred-epoch figure is not a floor: at epoch 99 the
gap is roughly a hundred times the residual it settles at, though both numbers are
imprecise enough that the ratio is an order of magnitude, not a measured constant
(\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S3.2).

Two cautions on reading the table. The points after epoch 124 are all within one
final-epoch seed standard deviation of each other ($\finalEpochSd$), so the decay is
established to about epoch 124 and the later entries are ordered noise rather
than a measured continuation: the claim that the series is \emph{monotone} holds
only of the points printed, not of the underlying trajectory. And the entries at
124, 199 and 250--299 are small differences of quantities whose own intervals are
wide relative to their distance from zero, which is why no ratio is quoted as a
measurement.

\emph{Convergence test.} The tail mean over epochs 250--299 is $\tailMean$ with a
95\% $t$ interval of $[\tailMeanLo, \tailMeanHi]$, which \emph{includes zero}:
three of five seeds are positive ($t = \tailT$, $p = \tailP$; Wilcoxon
$p = \tailWilcoxonP$). The tail is therefore indistinguishable from zero at five
seeds, and no positive residual is claimed. What the data do support is the
equivalence test in \S\ref{sec:equivalence}, which does not depend on the interval
excluding zero.

\emph{What ``converges'' is doing here, and what it is not.} The word appears
elsewhere in this paper and carries more than the design delivers. A
limit is not observed; a tail is. The measured statement is that the five-seed
mean gap is below the $0.002$ margin from about epoch 211 and that the tail is
indistinguishable from zero at five seeds. A flat tail is also what a very slow
decay looks like, and no test here separates the two: the stationarity result
below cannot, because a ten-epoch window at five seeds cannot detect a change of a
size that would still matter. The paper therefore reports a bounded residual, not
a convergence rate, and does not quote $\tailMean$ as a floor.

\emph{Stationarity.} A paired test of epochs 290--299 against 280--289 gives
$\statPairedDiff$ ($p = \statP$; Wilcoxon $\statWilcoxonP$;
$\statSeedsLateHigher$ of five seeds higher). The
largest single step in the last ten epochs is $\statMaxStep$, or $\statMaxStepShare$ per cent of the
final-epoch seed standard deviation (\texttt{long\_horizon\_stationarity.csv}).
The trajectory has stopped resolving above population noise, so 300 epochs
suffices here and 100 does not. But "stops resolving" is a statement about
the measurement, not about the process.

\emph{The curve fit, reported and not trusted.} AIC and BIC prefer
decay-to-constant over exponential decay decisively ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 23.9$
from the peak), but the constant it estimates is \emph{negative} with an interval
excluding zero while the observed tail is positive: a single exponential cannot
represent a series that first rises, so the constant absorbs the misfit rather
than estimating an asymptote (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S3.1;
\texttt{long\_horizon\_decay\_fits.csv}). The comparison answers which curve fits
better, not what the asymptote is.

\emph{What this establishes.} That the exposure gap at the shipped configuration
is a cold-start property that disappears, and that a hundred-epoch figure
describes a transient. Not that the mechanism is the one H3 offers, since the
cell that was meant to test it turns out at this horizon to be the same process
running more slowly (\S\ref{sec:sensitivity}), nor that convergence holds
elsewhere, since the three-hundred-epoch run exists only at the shipped cell.

\subsection{The convergence claim as an equivalence test}
\label{sec:equivalence}

"Below $0.001$ and indistinguishable from flat" is not a statement with a defined
meaning, so it is replaced by one. The margin was declared at $0.002$ Gini before
the test was run (\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E5), and the replicate is the
seed, not the epoch: the fifty tail values are one trajectory, so their spread is
not the sampling scale.

Two one-sided tests against $\pm 0.002$ both reject. With a seed sd of
$0.000340$, a standard error of $0.000152$ and four degrees of freedom,
$t(\mu > -0.002) = 14.7$ and $t(\mu < +0.002) = 11.6$; the 90\% interval over
seeds is $[-0.000094, +0.000555]$, wholly inside the margin. The tail is
\emph{equivalent} to zero at a pre-declared margin, which is a stronger and more
checkable statement than an interval that happens to include zero, and it is the
one claim here that the tail supports: the 95\% interval itself includes zero
(\S\ref{sec:coldstart}).

The same test gives a better summary of a transient than any single-epoch value:
the epoch at which the gap first stays below the margin for the remainder of the
run, per seed, is 245, 262, 198, 223 and 211. That range — about 200 to 260
epochs, not the 120 to 200 the pre-registration estimated — is the quantity to
quote when a reader asks how long the transient lasts.

\subsection{The institutional grid was measured in the inert regime}
\label{sec:h4regime}

One finding in this session has no pre-registration and belongs in the main text
anyway, because it changes what H4 means. The institutional comparison in
Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses} was generated in October at
\texttt{admissible\_fraction} $= 0.2$. That is the \emph{inert} gate: the
configuration \S\ref{sec:design} describes as one where $\theta$ never truncates a
shortlist and the market never responds to its own outcomes. The shipped value is
now $0.02$.

Both statements are verifiable from the repository. Re-running seeds 42--46 at
$0.2$ reproduces all 200 rows of \texttt{data/processed/institutional/grid.csv}
exactly, to the last digit, on \texttt{welfare\_common\_a},
\texttt{welfare\_diff\_residual} and \texttt{gini\_a}. Re-running the same seeds at
the shipped $0.02$ does not: the optimum cell reads $-17.23$ against the committed
$+64.62$. The regime, not the code and not the seeds, is the whole difference.

So the interior optimum H4 reports is a property of a market that does not learn
from its own clearing. That much was established by reproducing the committed
grid; the next question is whether the optimum survives at the gate the paper
actually runs, and it does not.

\emph{The grid re-run at the shipped gate.} The full ten-by-four grid was run at
\texttt{admissible\_fraction} $= 0.02$ on the same five seeds
(\texttt{data/processed/rigor/h4\_shipped\_gate/}). **No cell of the forty beats
Environment A.** At $c = 0.01$ the difference is close to monotone decreasing in
subgraph size, from $-0.23$ at size $0.02$ to $-40.22$ at $1.00$, and the cell
that was the optimum under the inert gate sits seventh of ten at $-17.23$. At
$c = 0.05$, $0.10$ and $0.25$ the best cell is negative as well ($-0.25$, $-0.28$,
$-0.37$).

This result was not pre-registered, and that matters for how much weight it
carries: it is recorded in \texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md} E6 with that label, on
the reasoning that a first measurement of a quantity with no prior is weak
evidence but not biased evidence, and that presenting it as a prediction would be
worse than the breach. It is also the only measurement of this quantity.

The reading is therefore plain, and it costs the paper one of its two results.
H4's interior optimum is a property of the regime where the threshold gate never
binds and the market never responds to its own outcomes. At the configuration the
rest of the paper runs, Environment B loses everywhere and loses more as it grows,
and the thin-market penalty dominates the information gain at every size tested.
The paper does not search for a cost or a size at which B wins; that search is
what the pre-registration discipline exists to prevent, and the honest report is a
refutation in the shipped regime alongside a positive result in a regime the paper
does not otherwise use.

\subsection{Robustness across the parameter sweep}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The hypotheses above were each measured at one setting of four modeling knobs.
This subsection asks where they hold, which is why the paper can report a size
for the exposure gap at all, since one cell cannot separate a property of the
mechanism from a property of its corner. Twelve cells, one knob moving at a time,
four claims fixed with their thresholds before the cells were read
(Table~\ref{tab:sens}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\input{tables/sensitivity}
\caption{The sensitivity grid: one knob at one level per row, the rest at shipped
values, five seeds, 100 epochs. Gap is the paired exposure difference, with its
95\% BCa bounds and its 95\% Student-$t$ bounds printed side by side, because the

holds on both constructions in all twelve cells.}
\label{tab:sens}

\emph{Holds everywhere.} The H3 ordering holds in all twelve cells, positive with
an interval excluding zero, reversing nowhere (C1). It holds on both
constructions: every cell's Student-$t$ interval excludes zero as well as every
cell's BCa interval, and the two are printed side by side
(Table~\ref{tab:sens}). The gap is small by the
pre-fixed ceiling in all twelve (C2), though the largest cell's upper bound
reaches $+0.0665$ and is the weakest case. Clearing is non-degenerate in all
twelve (C4). Neither the ordering nor the existence of a clearing market is an
artifact of the shipped corner.

\emph{What does not, at this horizon.} One cell fails the reach claim at 100
epochs: at the lowest chemistry noise the ranker reaches $0.4616$ of agents
against $0.9566$ shipped, both fractions of the population (C3). The same
comparison in counts is $462$ agents against $957$, out of $N = 1000$; reach is
reported as a fraction everywhere else in this paper and is a fraction here. The
gap there is small and positive ($+0.0097$), and the reading the sweep supports
is that the cell is at a different point in the same process rather than at the
end of a different one.

\emph{And what a longer horizon does to that reading.} The cell was then run to
300 epochs (\texttt{data/processed/rigor/c4\_shock0.5/}; five seeds, 1 thread).
Reach climbs from $0.041$ at epoch 0 to $0.269$ at epoch 49, $0.457$ at epoch 99,
and $0.490$ at epoch 299. The gap peaks at $+0.0565$ at epoch 9 and decays to
$-0.0012$ at epoch 299 — proportionally as complete a decay as the shipped cell's,
from a peak a fifth as large.

So $0.4616$ was reach at \emph{epoch 99}, not a ceiling. The sweep therefore
does not identify a cell where the mechanism is absent, only one where it runs
more slowly — which withdraws the conclusion-reversing claim this paper made and
leaves the mechanism untested rather than vindicated, since a cell where reach
climbs slowly is not a cell where reach is held fixed. At 100 epochs the filter
learns coverage in every cell, at rates spanning a factor of two, and none was run
long enough to show where the convergence it drives stops.

\emph{How large.} The gap ranges from $+0.0097$ to $+0.0470$, a factor of about
five, with no change in sign; the shipped cell sits mid-range. Quote the range
with its condition, or the shipped cell with its regime, never $+0.025$ as
``the'' gap.

\emph{A documented condition that does not reproduce.} The configuration states,
and a guard test pins, that the hypergamy margin $\delta$ has power only when
$\beta/\alpha \geq 2$. Re-measuring shortlist overlap at the shipped
heterogeneity and noise reverses the ordering: $\beta/\alpha = 1$ gives $\delta$
\emph{more} power than $3$, and all three levels clear the threshold the
guardrail treats as meaningful (\texttt{docs/STATS.md} \S4.2;
\texttt{sensitivity/delta\_power.csv}). The guard test is not wrong; it was
measured on a noise-free homogeneous population, and the rule of thumb describes
that population rather than the one the experiments run on.

What the sweep does show is that $\delta$ moves no headline: the gap is $+0.0229$
at $0$, $+0.0260$ at $0.5$ and $+0.0263$ at $1.0$, a spread of $0.0034$ against
intervals about $0.012$ wide, while the weight ratio moves it twice as far and
chemistry noise four times as far. The defensible statement is that no claim here
rests on $\delta$ at any weight ratio tested, not that the results require $\beta/\alpha
\geq 2$.

\emph{Refuted?} The sweep refutes the unconditional form of the cold-start
explanation and the stated $\beta/\alpha$ condition. It does not refute H3's
ordering, the one result that survives every cell.
