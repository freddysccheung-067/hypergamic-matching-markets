# Long-horizon ablation: does the NCF–oracle gap converge, and to what?

Generated by `scripts/run_ablation.py` on 2026-10-24/25 and analysed by
`scripts/analyse_long_horizon.py`.

    python scripts/run_ablation.py --config experiments/base.yaml \
        --seeds 42,43,44,45,46 --epochs 300 \
        --output-dir data/processed/rigor/long_horizon/
    python scripts/analyse_long_horizon.py

## Regime

**φ = 0.02 (the live gate, the shipped value), 300 epochs.** N = 1000, k = 20,
`experiments/base.yaml` as shipped, seeds 42–46. This is `ablation_h100/` run
three times longer: the first 100 epochs are identical row for row to that
directory, which is the check that the horizon is the only thing that differs.

The point of the horizon is that THESIS H3 quotes the gap at epoch 99 and calls it
a cold-start effect without saying what it converges to. A 100-epoch run cannot
answer that, because at epoch 99 the gap is still falling.

## Result

Mean NCF−oracle Gini gap over five seeds, by epoch:

| epoch | 0 | 6 | 19 | 49 | 99 | 124 | 199 | 299 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gap | +0.2633 | **+0.3055** | +0.2869 | +0.1718 | +0.0241 | +0.0072 | +0.0025 | +0.00044 |

It peaks at epoch 6 and then decays to essentially nothing.

**Asymptote.** The mean over the last 50 epochs (250–299) is **+0.00037**, with a
95% t interval of **+0.000013 to +0.000735**. All five seeds are positive over
that window; p = 0.045 on the t test and 0.063 on Wilcoxon. The honest reading is
that the gap is small and positive but barely distinguishable from zero — and
certainly not the +0.02 or so that a 100-epoch run would lead a reader to expect
as a floor.

**Curve fits, and why they are not quoted as the answer.** AIC and BIC both prefer
`K + A·exp(−λt)` to `A·exp(−λt)` (ΔBIC = 23.9 from the peak), but the K it fits is
**negative** (−0.0079 from the peak, −0.011 from epoch 0) where the observed tail
is positive. A single exponential cannot describe a series that rises before it
decays, so the constant absorbs the misfit rather than estimating an asymptote.
The non-parametric tail mean is therefore the number to quote. See
`../long_horizon_decay_fits.csv` for both fits, both windows.

**Stationarity.** The NCF arm is stationary by epoch 299. A paired test of epochs
290–299 against 280–289 gives +0.0001 (paired t p = 0.32, Wilcoxon p = 0.31, three
of five seeds higher); the largest single step in the last ten epochs is 0.0004,
which is 3.7% of the seed sd at the final epoch (0.0111). So 300 epochs is enough
for this run — and 100 is not: at epoch 99 the gap is still 55× its terminal value.

## Quoting these numbers

Every figure is a mean over five seeds. The tail interval rests on five
independent populations, and five is enough to say "small" but only marginally
enough to say "positive". Quote the interval, not the point. See
`docs/STATS.md` §3.
